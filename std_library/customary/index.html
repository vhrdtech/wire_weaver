Basic

Imperial and US customary units¶

exact_units has a marker for every imperial, US customary and other non-SI unit still in use (feature customary, opt-in: not part of quantities or std, so an API without them pays nothing for them; ww_si's customary turns it on): inches, pounds, gallons, psi, Btu, horsepower, slugs, knots, mils. Each one's scale is the exact rational of SI its legal definition gives, with the source cited in its docs, so a value in pound-force seconds converts to newton seconds exactly, or doesn't convert at all.

use exact_units::{Fixed, prefix::{Milli, One, Pico}, units::{Newton, PoundForce, Second, Times}};

type LbfSeconds = Fixed<Times<PoundForce, Second>, Milli, i32>;

let impulse = LbfSeconds::new(2000); // 2 lbf·s
// 1 lbf·s is 4.4482216152605 N·s exactly
assert_eq!(
    impulse.convert::<Times<Newton, Second>, Pico, i64>(),
    Some(Fixed::new(8_896_443_230_521))
);
// 8.896443230521 N·s is not a whole number of mN·s: refused, not rounded
assert_eq!(impulse.convert::<Times<Newton, Second>, Milli, i64>(), None);

On the wire a Fixed in a customary unit is its integer, like any other Fixed: the unit is in the API, not in the bytes. The markers are zero-size types, and convert costs what Fixed::to costs: its factor is worked out at compile time.

No bare ambiguous names¶

"Gallon", "ton", "ounce", "BTU" or "horsepower" each mean several units that differ by up to a fifth. None of them is a marker: there is GallonUs and GallonImperial, TonShort, TonLong and Tonne, BtuIt and BtuTh. A test checks that no marker is named by a word of the table below, and that every unit called after such a word is in its group. The one exception is Degree, the angle: SI names it so, and a temperature degree is always "degree Fahrenheit" or "degree Celsius".

Mass and force¶

A pound of mass and a pound of force are different dimensions, and so different types:

Marker Dimension Definition
PoundAvdp mass 0.453 592 37 kg (1959)
PoundForce force lb · g_n = 4.448 221 615 260 5 N
Kip force 1000 lbf
Poundal force lb · ft/s² = 0.138 254 954 376 N
Slug mass lbf · s²/ft = 14.593 902 937.. kg

Fixed<PoundForce, ..>::convert::<PoundAvdp, ..>() doesn't compile, and a dynamic client refuses the same. A slug is a mass of about 32.174 lb (g_n in ft/s²), not a pound.

Temperatures¶

Degrees Fahrenheit and Rankine are points on a scale, not units: Point<Fahrenheit, Fixed<Kelvin, Rational<5, 9>, T>> and Point<Rankine, ..> (see points). A temperature difference in °F is a Fixed<Kelvin, Rational<5, 9>, T>.

Mercury, water, Btu and calories¶

A pressure in inches or millimetres of mercury or of water depends on the density of the liquid, which depends on its temperature. The markers say which one they are:

  • conventional, a defined density and g_n: InchOfMercuryConventional (13 595.1 kg/m³, ISO 80000-4), MillimetreOfMercuryConventional (133.322 387 415 Pa), InchOfWaterConventional (1000 kg/m³); and Torr, 101 325/760 Pa, and the EU's legal MillimetreOfMercuryEu, 133.322 Pa;
  • at a stated temperature, InchOfMercury32f, InchOfWater39f: measured.

The same for Btu and calories: the International Table and thermochemical ones are defined, those at a temperature or "mean" are measured. A measured unit's scale is the value NIST SP 811 prints, exact in this crate by convention only; UnitExpr::Named's provenance is Provenance::Conventional for them, Defined for every other unit. An API model carries it too (as ww_self::MarkerDef::ConventionalUnit, a unit with its source, while a defined unit stays a plain MarkerDef::Unit and pays nothing for it), for these and for an API crate's own unit! lines with conventional(..), so a client knows which scales are conventions:

The Mars Climate Orbiter case¶

In 1999 a ground program wrote thruster impulse in pound-force seconds where the navigation software expected newton seconds, and the orbiter was lost. With WireWeaver the unit is part of the API, so a client either converts exactly or refuses; it never reads the number as another unit's.

  • Typed (Rust): Fixed::convert as above; a Quantity<Times<PoundForce, Second>> is the same value with its prefix sent along.
  • Dynamic client: DynQuantity::convert(&DynUnit::from_name("Times<Newton, Second>")?) gives 8896443230521 p(N·s) for 2 lbf·s (an integer gets the largest SI prefix that keeps it whole, a float stays a float); a unit of another dimension is an error ("dimension .. is not ..").
  • Python: q.convert("Times<Newton, Second>"), on a quantity read from the device:
q = api.impulse_q.decode(raw)       # 2 lbf·s
si = q.convert("Times<Newton, Second>")
assert str(si) == "8896443230521 p(N·s)"
q.convert("Times<PoundAvdp, Second>")  # ValueError: dimension .. is not ..

tests/customary runs the case through a device: a Fixed, a Quantity and an AnyQuantity in lbf·s. The dynamic client reads the Fixed field with its unit (2000 m(lbf·s), {:#} 2 lbf·s), converts it to N·s exactly, and refuses 4 N·s written into it (not a whole number of m(lbf·s)), as for the others (in clients).

  • Evolution checker: an API that changes Fixed<Times<PoundForce, Second>, ..> to Fixed<Times<Newton, Second>, ..> keeps the bytes, and the checker calls it breaking with the factor (1 on the old version is 4.448222), also at connect time; see units compared by meaning (EVO-17).

Sources¶

Every definition is from one of these documents, each read in full; a unit's docs say which and where:

  • Refinement of values for the yard and the pound, Federal Register 24 FR 5348 (1 Jul 1959), reprinted in NBS Misc. Publ. 233 (1960)
  • NIST Special Publication 811, Guide for the Use of the International System of Units (SI), Appendix B
  • NIST Handbook 44 (2026), Appendix C, General Tables of Units of Measurement
  • Weights and Measures Act 1985 (c. 72), Schedule 1
  • 3rd CGPM (1901), Resolution 2
  • BIPM, The International System of Units (SI), 8th edition (2006)
  • Federal Register 85 FR 62698 (5 Oct 2020), deprecation of the U.S. survey foot
  • Council Directive 80/181/EEC of 20 December 1979 on units of measurement
  • 21 CFR 101.9 Nutrition labeling of food
