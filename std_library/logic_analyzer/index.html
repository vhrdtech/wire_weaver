Logic analyzer and signal generator (design)¶

Approved, skeletons built

STD-21 ww_logic_analyzer and STD-22 ww_signal_generator, the design pass the IO family asked for, approved by Roman on 14 Oct 2026 with the answers in Review answers. Both crates exist as skeletons (ww_stdlib/ww_logic_analyzer, ww_stdlib/ww_signal_generator); their sources are the API description. This page stays as the reasoning: the design may still improve once a device uses it, edge cases are hard to imagine before. Everything here follows the family's conventions (errors, streams, capabilities, ww_si values); only what is new is written out.

What the two crates are for¶

ww_logic_analyzer samples many digital channels at one fixed rate, with a common time base, from an MCU with a DMA-able port or a PIO up to a dedicated analyzer. ww_signal_generator is the reverse: it drives many digital channels from a pattern at a fixed rate (PatternGenerator), or an analog output from a waveform (WaveformGenerator, a DAC).

They exist next to ww_gpio because GPIO is one pin, one call, no timing; here the whole point is timing: every sample of every channel lands on one grid, and many pins switch on the same tick.

Crate Trait What it is for Who implements it
ww_logic_analyzer LogicAnalyzer Triggered capture into device RAM, read out after; lossless at any rate the hardware can sample Every analyzer
LogicStream Continuous capture streamed while the link keeps up Devices with a fast link or sparse signals
ww_signal_generator PatternGenerator Digital channels driven from a pattern, one-shot or repeated; replays an analyzer capture MCUs with DMA-to-GPIO or a PIO, pattern generators
WaveformGenerator channel[] of DAC outputs: sine, square, triangle, sawtooth, DC, arbitrary tables Devices with a DAC

Protocol decoding (UART, I2C, SPI, CAN on top of captured edges) stays on the host, where sigrok/PulseView and our own decoders live. A device that decodes a protocol itself implements that protocol's monitor trait (I2cMonitor, UartMonitor, SpiMonitor) instead.

Encodings: Raw and Transitions¶

These are the reason the crate exists. Both are used by the analyzer (to read captures and to stream) and by the pattern generator (to load patterns), so a capture can be replayed without conversion.

Raw: every sample, bit-packed¶

Samples one after another, each a word of width bits (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32, the smallest the device can give for the channel count, see RawLayout). Bit i of a word is the i-th channel of the configuration's channel list; sub-byte words are packed into a byte starting at bit 0 (sample 0 in bits 0..width), multi-byte words are little-endian. This is what a DMA from a GPIO port produces with no CPU work (a port is 8, 16 or 32 bits) and what sigrok calls unitsize for whole bytes.

Raw is the right encoding for busy signals (a fast clock sampled near its Nyquist rate), for devices with no CPU time to spare, and for the pattern generator's DMA. Its size is samples × width / 8 regardless of what the signals do.

Transitions: only the edges, per channel¶

For each channel: its level at the start of the range and the sample indices where it toggles, as a DeltaOfDelta<'i, u32> (SW-31, in APIs since SW-50). Every listed index is an edge of that channel (levels are implied: each one toggles), so no level bits are stored. A channel that keeps a steady rhythm (a clock, a UART at a fixed baud, PWM at a fixed duty) costs about one bit per edge; a quiet channel costs its start level and an empty list.

Measured with a scratch encoder on this branch (synthetic captures, DeltaOfDelta / Delta from shrink_wrap as they are on master):

Capture Raw Merged + DoD Per channel, DoD Per channel, Delta Per channel, DoD, chunks of 128 edges
SPI 1 MHz at 8 MS/s, 4 ch, 512 bytes 18944 B 5233 B 4584 B 14080 B 5607 B
same SPI on 8 channels (4 idle) 37888 B 9344 B 4588 B 14084 B 5607 B
UART 115200 at 1 MS/s, 512 bytes 7292 B 3149 B 2803 B 3148 B 3019 B
UART 115200 at 24 MS/s, 512 bytes 175000 B 4304 B 3954 B 4886 B 4168 B
I2C 400 kHz at 4 MS/s, 2 ch, 320 bytes 8080 B 2302 B 2076 B 8196 B 2733 B
PWM 1 kHz 30 % at 1 MS/s, 100 ms 12500 B 427 B 402 B 402 B 416 B
8 ch: 1 kHz clock + slow counter, 100 ms 100000 B 232 B 51 B 423 B 74 B

"Merged" is the alternative layout: one index list of every sample where any channel changed, plus the new level word (one bit per channel) for each. Per channel wins in every case and doesn't grow with idle channels, which is why it is the proposal. DeltaOfDelta beats Delta wherever a clock is involved (a constant interval is 1 bit); both lose on PWM with an uneven duty (intervals alternate 300 / 700, every change of the interval costs a 16-bit bucket), which is still 3 % of raw. The last column is what replies and stream items actually cost: each chunk restarts the code with its first index at full width.

What it gives: the sample rate stops being the limit, the edge rate is. A UART sampled at 24 MS/s for clean edge timing costs as much as at 1 MS/s; a mostly idle bus can be streamed at a rate whose raw form no link carries.

Device side. Transitions are made from raw samples by the device's CPU: XOR of consecutive words gives the changed channels, one pass per read. A device that can't spare the CPU lists only Raw; one that samples through edge-capturing hardware (timer input capture, a PIO program that counts) can store transitions directly and list only Transitions, the host converts. shrink_wrap's DeltaOfDelta serializes from a slice, so a device collects up to a chunk's worth of indices (128 × u32, 512 bytes of RAM per channel being encoded) and serializes them in one go; the worst case of 128 indices is 128 × 37 bits = 592 bytes, which fits a reply.

Indices are u32 sample numbers counted from the capture's first sample (LogicAnalyzer) or from the start of the stream (LogicStream). At 100 MS/s a u32 wraps after 43 s; the stream's indices wrap and the host unwraps them like any fixed-width timestamp (the D6 rule of the family); captures longer than 2³² samples are read in ranges and each chunk carries its own base (see TransitionsChunk).

LogicAnalyzer: triggered capture into RAM¶

Same shape as AdcCapture, so a generic tool that knows one knows the other: arm with a configuration, poll the status, read the data out in pieces, abort.

#[ww_trait]
pub trait LogicAnalyzer {
    /// Channels, rates, memory, encodings, triggers.
    fn capabilities() -> LaCapabilities<'i>;

    /// Arms a capture (replacing the previous one and its data) and returns what was actually set.
    fn capture(config: CaptureConfig<'i>) -> Result<CaptureActual, LaError>;

    /// Arms again with the last configuration (repeated captures of the same event).
    fn rearm() -> Result<CaptureActual, LaError>;

    /// Where the capture stands.
    fn status() -> CaptureStatus;

    /// Samples `range` of a finished capture as raw words, at most `MAX_READ_LEN` bytes; returns how many samples
    /// it holds (a whole number of words, possibly fewer than asked).
    fn read_raw(range: SampleRange) -> Result<RawChunk<'i>, LaError>;

    /// Edges of `channel` from `from` on, up to one chunk; the reply says up to which sample it looked.
    fn read_transitions(channel: u8, from: u32) -> Result<TransitionsChunk<'i>, LaError>;

    /// Stops an armed or running capture and drops its data.
    fn abort();
}

Notes against the sketch in io_family (configure, arm, status, read(offset, len, encoding)):

  • capture(config) arms, as in AdcCapture; rearm() covers the scope-like "single, again, again" without sending the configuration each time.
  • Reads are in samples, not bytes, and split by encoding: a byte offset means nothing for transitions, whose size depends on the signal.
  • read_transitions is per channel. It is the cheapest for the device (one bit of every word, one pass, a fixed buffer of indices) and lets a host fetch only the channels it shows. A host fetching all channels sends one call per channel in one multi-call (CLT-15). Each reply holds edges in from..scanned_to; the next call starts at scanned_to. A device stops at 128 edges or when the reply would overflow, whichever comes first.

Configuration.

pub struct CaptureConfig<'i> {
    /// Channel numbers ([LaCapabilities::channels]), in the bit order of raw words.
    pub channels: MaxLen<RefVec<'i, u8>, MAX_CHANNELS>,
    /// Samples per second.
    pub rate: Hertz,
    /// Samples to keep in total, `pre_trigger` included.
    pub samples: u32,
    /// Samples to keep from before the trigger.
    pub pre_trigger: u32,
    pub trigger: Trigger<'i>,
    /// Input threshold where it is adjustable; `None` leaves it.
    pub threshold: Option<Volt>,
    /// Pulse the trigger output when the capture triggers.
    pub trigger_out: bool,
}

CaptureActual returns the real rate and samples (the memory may hold fewer at this channel count), and the RawLayout (width per sample) raw reads will have.

Storage. The device decides how it keeps a capture: as raw words (most MCUs, DMA into a buffer) or as transitions (edge capture hardware, or a CPU compressing on the fly like Saleae devices do). With transitions in RAM, samples can be far larger than the memory in raw words; the capture ends when samples are taken or the memory fills, and CaptureStatus::Done says how many samples it covers. LaCapabilities::storage says which, so a host can offer long captures where they make sense. Either storage is read in either encoding the device lists.

Status.

pub enum CaptureStatus {
    Idle,
    /// Sampling the pre-trigger part, waiting for the trigger.
    Armed,
    /// Triggered at `t`, taking the rest.
    Triggered { t: Second },
    /// Done. Sample 0 was taken at `t_first`; the trigger fired at sample `trigger_at`. `samples` may be fewer than
    /// asked when transition storage filled up.
    Done { t_first: Second, trigger_at: Option<u32>, samples: u32 },
}

Times are ww_si::Second of the device clock (ww_timebase::Clock), as everywhere in the family; the time of sample k is t_first + k / rate with the actual rate. trigger_at is a sample index, exact to the sample, rather than a second timestamp.

Triggers¶

pub enum Trigger<'i> {
    /// Right away.
    Now,
    /// Stage after stage, each one met after the one before; the capture triggers when the last one is met.
    Stages(MaxLen<RefVec<'i, TriggerStage<'i>>, MAX_STAGES>),
    /// On the external trigger input.
    External { edge: Edge },
}

pub enum TriggerStage<'i> {
    /// Every condition holds on the same sample.
    Pattern(MaxLen<RefVec<'i, ChannelCondition>, MAX_CHANNELS>),
    /// A UART frame (8 data bits, LSB first, idle high) on `channel` at `baud` whose data matches `value` under
    /// `mask`; met at the stop bit.
    UartByte { channel: u8, baud: u32, value: u8, mask: u8 },
    /// An I2C address byte on `scl` / `sda` matching `address`, of a read (`Some(true)`), a write (`Some(false)`)
    /// or either; met at the ACK bit.
    I2cAddress { scl: u8, sda: u8, address: ww_i2c::I2cAddress, read: Option<bool> },
    /// The first byte on `data` (MOSI or MISO) after `cs` goes low, clocked by `sck` in SPI `mode` (0-3), matching
    /// `value` under `mask`; met at its last bit.
    SpiFirstByte { cs: u8, sck: u8, data: u8, mode: u8, msb_first: bool, value: u8, mask: u8 },
}

pub struct ChannelCondition {
    pub channel: u8,
    pub when: Condition,
}

/// `ww_repr = u3`
pub enum Condition { Low, High, Rising, Falling, AnyEdge }

/// `ww_repr = u2`
pub enum Edge { Rising, Falling, Any }

The Pattern stage is sigrok's trigger model, which covers the cases the family named: level (one condition High), edge (Rising), pattern (several Low / High in one stage), and an edge qualified by a pattern ("CLK rising while CS low"). Stages give sequences ("CS falls, then MOSI rises") on hardware that has them; LaCapabilities::trigger says how many stages and which conditions work (an MCU with a pin-change interrupt does one stage of edges on a few channels; an FPGA analyzer does several stages of anything).

Protocol stages (review: wanted in the first version, they make debugging much easier) match a decoded event instead of a sample: a UART byte, an I2C address with its R/W bit, the first SPI byte after CS. They are matched on the decoded stream next to the capture (on rePort in the PIO program or the firmware), decoding only as much as the match needs; LaCapabilities says which stage kinds work. Being stages, they combine with each other and with patterns ("CS low, then the UART byte 0x55"), which already gives sequences. Data patterns over several bytes (UartBytes { pattern }, an I2C register write), more protocols (CAN identifier), pulse-width and counted stages are appended as TriggerStage variants when a device needs them; the enum is shaped for that, every stage is self-contained. Unknown variants from newer hosts are handled by the automatic #[future_version] variant (SW-47, ww-errors-plan) like every enum of the family: Trigger, TriggerStage, Condition and Shape get no hand-written Unknown.

Trigger output. CaptureConfig::trigger_out: bool drives the device's trigger output when the capture triggers, so another analyzer, a scope or a PatternGenerator on another device starts on the same edge. A common trigger shared across devices and functions (routing, sync-based triggers at a shared time) belongs to ww_timebase (review answer 5); until it exists, this bool and Trigger::External are the wire-level link, and the timestamps line up through ww_timebase's clock sync (below).

LogicStream: continuous capture¶

#[ww_trait]
pub trait LogicStream {
    /// Captured data, in the configured encoding.
    stream!(data: StreamChunk<'i>);

    fn stream_capabilities() -> StreamCapabilities<'i>;

    /// Sets up streaming and returns what was set. Sideband `Open` starts sampling, `Close` stops it.
    fn configure_stream(config: StreamConfig<'i>) -> Result<StreamActual, LaError>;

    fn stream_state() -> StreamState<'i>;
}

pub struct StreamConfig<'i> {
    pub channels: MaxLen<RefVec<'i, u8>, MAX_CHANNELS>,
    pub rate: Hertz,
    pub encoding: Encoding,
    /// Send a chunk at least this often even if it isn't full; `None` lets the device choose.
    pub flush_interval: Option<Second>,
    pub threshold: Option<Volt>,
}

pub enum StreamChunk<'i> {
    Raw(RawChunk<'i>),
    Transitions(TransitionsChunk<'i>),
}

It is the same convention as AdcStream (configure with a method, sideband Open / Close). With Raw the link must carry rate × width bits per second; with Transitions only the edges, so StreamCapabilities gives a max_raw_rate and a max_rate (what the sampler can do while the edge rate fits). Every chunk carries its first sample index, which lines chunks up on the timeline; what happens when the host can't keep up (counting lost chunks, backfill from device RAM) is ww-stream-reliability's, as for every stream in the family. Until it lands, a gap in the sample indices is what a host sees.

Types¶

/// A run of raw samples.
pub struct RawChunk<'i> {
    /// Index of the first sample.
    pub first: u32,
    pub samples: u32,
    /// Words as in [RawLayout].
    pub data: MaxLen<RefVec<'i, u8>, MAX_READ_LEN>,
}

/// How raw words are laid out: `width` bits per sample (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32).
pub struct RawLayout { pub width: u8 }

/// Edges of one channel in `from..scanned_to`.
pub struct TransitionsChunk<'i> {
    pub channel: u8,
    /// Level at sample `from`.
    pub level: Level,
    pub from: u32,
    /// No more edges before this sample; the next chunk of this channel starts here.
    pub scanned_to: u32,
    /// Samples where the channel toggles, ascending, in `from..scanned_to`.
    pub edges: DeltaOfDelta<'i, u32>,
}

pub enum Encoding { Raw, Transitions }

Level is ww_gpio::Level (1 bit), so the crates share it. ChannelInfo (in LaCapabilities::channels) has a name, the threshold range if adjustable, and gpio_pin: Option<u8>, the index of the same pin in the device's ww_gpio::Bank if it has one.

Errors: one plain LaError with the family's first four (Unsupported, InUse, NoIoLevel, Refused) and InvalidArgument, WrongMode (reading a capture that isn't done), NoSuchChannel, Busy (a stream runs while a capture is asked for, or the other way round, on devices that share the sampler).

PatternGenerator¶

#[ww_trait]
pub trait PatternGenerator {
    fn capabilities() -> PgCapabilities<'i>;

    /// Sets channels, rate, repetition and start condition; returns what was set. Drops a loaded pattern.
    fn configure(config: PgConfig<'i>) -> Result<PgActual, PgError>;

    /// Pattern data, appended in order. `Open` starts a new pattern, `Close` marks it complete.
    #[credit]
    sink!(pattern: StreamChunk<'i>);

    /// Starts output now, or waits for `PgConfig::start`. Playing before the pattern is complete streams it: the
    /// device plays what it has while the sink keeps filling, and stops with `Underrun` if the host falls behind.
    fn start() -> Result<(), PgError>;

    /// Stops output; channels go to their idle levels.
    fn stop();

    fn status() -> PgStatus;
}

Replaying a capture. The sink takes ww_logic_analyzer::StreamChunk: Raw(RawChunk) or Transitions(TransitionsChunk) (each channel's edges, in chunks), the very items LogicAnalyzer::read_raw / read_transitions and LogicStream return. A host replays a capture by reading it in the encoding the generator accepts (PgCapabilities::encodings) and sending the chunks to the sink, with the capture's rate and channel mapping in PgConfig. A helper in the std part converts between the two encodings for devices that take only one.

Raw is what a DMA into a GPIO set/reset register plays (STM32 BSRR, a PIO out pins), so most MCUs take it. Transitions suits devices that time edges with timer compare or a PIO delay loop; for them a sparse pattern at a high rate fits in little memory.

Configuration.

pub struct PgConfig<'i> {
    /// Output channels ([PgCapabilities::channels]), in the bit order of raw words and as the channel numbers of
    /// transitions.
    pub channels: MaxLen<RefVec<'i, u8>, MAX_CHANNELS>,
    pub rate: Hertz,
    pub repeat: Repeat,
    /// Levels before the start and after the end, bit `i` for `channels[i]`.
    pub idle: u32,
    pub start: Start,
}

pub enum Repeat { Once, Times(u32), Forever }

pub enum Start {
    /// On `start()`.
    Now,
    /// On the external trigger input.
    External { edge: Edge },
    /// When this device's `LogicAnalyzer` triggers: a stimulus and its response on one time base.
    AnalyzerTrigger,
}

PgStatus: Idle, Loading { samples }, Ready, Running { repeat }, Done { t_start: Second }, Underrun. The sink's credit is the device's free pattern memory, so a host can't overrun it; streaming a pattern longer than the memory works as long as the link keeps up.

Pins. While the generator runs, its pins belong to it: ww_gpio's Pin::set_mode / set_output_level on them answer InUse; reading their level still works. Same rule for analyzer channels that are also outputs elsewhere: sampling is passive, so a LogicAnalyzer may watch pins the device drives (useful to see one's own output), and InUse is only for pins the analyzer would have to reconfigure (a threshold, a pull).

WaveformGenerator¶

#[ww_trait]
pub trait WaveformGenerator {
    ww_impl!(channel[]: WaveformChannel);

    fn capabilities() -> WgCapabilities<'i>;

    /// Starts every channel in `channels` on the same sample clock tick, for phase-aligned outputs.
    fn start_together(channels: MaxLen<RefVec<'i, u8>, MAX_CHANNELS>) -> Result<(), WgError>;
}

#[ww_trait]
pub trait WaveformChannel {
    /// Starts or changes the output, returns what was set (frequency and levels as the DAC can do them).
    fn set(waveform: Waveform) -> Result<WaveformActual, WgError>;

    /// Stops and holds `level`.
    fn stop(level: Volt) -> Result<(), WgError>;

    fn state() -> WaveformState;

    /// Table of an `Arbitrary` waveform, appended in order; `Open` starts a new table, `Close` completes it.
    #[credit]
    sink!(table: MaxLen<RefVec<'i, u8>, MAX_TABLE_CHUNK>);
}

pub struct Waveform {
    pub shape: Shape,
    pub freq: Hertz,
    /// Peak to peak.
    pub amplitude: Volt,
    pub offset: Volt,
    /// 0..=65535 = 0..360°, against the other channels started together.
    pub phase: u16,
}

pub enum Shape {
    Sine,
    Square { duty: u16 },
    Triangle,
    Sawtooth { rising: bool },
    /// `offset` only, `freq` and `amplitude` ignored.
    Dc,
    /// The `table` sink's samples, played once per period (`freq` is the table's repetition rate), or once
    /// and then held when `once` is set.
    Arbitrary { format: SampleFormat, once: bool },
}

Amplitudes and offsets are ww_si::Volt, frequencies Hertz (D2, D3); WaveformActual returns what the DAC actually does (the frequency its sample clock divides to, the clamped levels). A square wave from a timer pin is ww_pwm, not this trait; WaveformGenerator is for DACs (an MCU's DAC with a DMA table, a DDS chip, a lab function generator). Sweeps, bursts, modulation and noise are appendable to Shape or as fields with defaults later.

Arbitrary tables are raw DAC codes in a SampleFormat like ww_adc's (U8, U16, Packed12, I16), with WgCapabilities giving the code-to-volt Linear per channel, the same pair ww_adc uses the other way round. A table played with once and fed faster than it plays is a streamed DAC output, the reverse of AdcStream.

How it composes with ww_gpio and ww_timebase¶

  • ww_gpio: channels of both crates name the same pin's ww_gpio::Bank index (gpio_pin), so a tool shows "LA channel 3 = PA5 = gpio pin 5". The generator takes pins over while running (InUse for ww_gpio); the analyzer only watches. ww_gpio::Level is the level type of both.
  • ww_timebase: every timestamp (CaptureStatus::Done::t_first, Triggered::t, PgStatus::Done::t_start) is device time from Clock::now()'s clock, and the sample clock runs from the same oscillator, so t_first + k / rate is exact against the rest of the device's data (an I2C transaction from I2cMonitor, an ADC capture). Two devices synced through Clock::configure_sync line their captures up through the time_map stream, which is enough for "which device saw it first" to a few µs; for sample-exact alignment, wire the trigger out of one to the trigger in of the other.
  • ww_adc: a mixed-signal capture is an AdcCapture and a LogicAnalyzer::capture armed with External triggers from one source, or AnalyzerTrigger-style coupling inside one device later (not in this version).

Host side (std)¶

Helpers in each crate's std part, not on the wire:

  • Capture: a whole capture as per-channel edge lists, filled from either encoding, with level_at(channel, sample), iteration over edges, conversion back to raw words and to transitions chunks for replay.
  • read_capture(client, config): arm, wait, read everything in the best encoding the device lists, as a multi-call per round of chunks.
  • VCD export (GTKWave, PulseView, sigrok-cli read it): a few dozen lines on top of Capture, no dependency.

Crate and version plan¶

Review answers¶

Roman, 14 Oct 2026: the design is accepted with these answers.

  1. Per-channel transitions: yes.
  2. DeltaOfDelta<u32> only: yes; another encoding is appended to Encoding if a real capture needs it.
  3. ww_adc's SampleFormat / Linear reused in ww_signal_generator now; they move with STD-23 if it moves them.
  4. rearm() stays.
  5. Trigger output as a bool for now; a common cross-device trigger belongs in ww_timebase.

Plus: protocol triggers in the first version, the simple ones (UART byte, I2C address + R/W, SPI first byte after CS: the UartByte, I2cAddress and SpiFirstByte stages), matched on the decoded stream next to the capture; multi-byte patterns later, as further stages; no hand-written Unknown variants, the automatic #[future_version] (SW-47) covers Trigger, TriggerStage, Condition and Shape; build the agreed core without over-building, the design improves once it is used.

Not in this version¶

Protocol stages beyond UART and I2C, pulse-width and counted triggers; a cross-device trigger (ww_timebase); per-channel sample rates (mixed-rate analyzers); on-device protocol decoding (use the monitor traits); analog channels in a logic analyzer (an AdcCapture next to it); sweeps, bursts and modulation in WaveformGenerator; sigrok session (.sr) export.
