Exact magnitudes¶

Every scale in exact_units is a Magnitude: one canonical, exact factor. A prefix, a power of ten or two, a fraction, a full-scale step, a degree in radians and the legal definition of the pound all have the same form, so two scales compare equal however they are written, and a conversion between them is worked out exactly at compile time.

use exact_units::{FixedScale, Magnitude, prefix::{Milli, Pow10, Rational}};

assert_eq!(Milli::SCALE, Pow10::<-3>::SCALE);
assert_eq!(Milli::SCALE, Rational::<1, 1000>::SCALE);
// the pound, 0.45359237 kg = 7 · 11 · 97 · 6073 · 10⁻⁸
const LB: Magnitude = Magnitude::ratio(45359237, 100_000_000);
assert_eq!(LB.to_ratio(), Some((45359237, 100_000_000)));

The form¶

A magnitude is a product of powers:

10^a · 2^b · π^c · ln2^d · ln10^e · ∏ pᵢ^kᵢ

over primes pᵢ other than 2 and 5, every exponent a whole or a half number. It is kept canonical: 2 and 5 appear only in a and b (5 = 10 · 2⁻¹), the other primes in ascending order with exponents that aren't 0, so equal magnitudes are equal field by field.

Spelled Magnitude
Micro, Pow10<-6> 10⁻⁶
Kibi, Pow2<10> 2¹⁰
Rational<1, 65535> (Duty16) 10⁻¹ · 2 · 3⁻¹ · 17⁻¹ · 257⁻¹
a degree in rad 10⁻¹ · 2⁻¹ · 3⁻² · π
a decibel in neper 10⁻¹ · 2⁻¹ · ln10
√2 (a noise density's root) 2^½

The form is closed under mul, div, powi and sqrt: products never round. Two things are refused, both a compile error in a const (MagnitudeError from the checked_ forms at run time): more than MAX_FACTORS (12) primes other than 2 and 5, and an exponent outside ±63½. The 171 imperial and US customary units of STD-72 take at most 7 such primes (the slug: 3, 7, 11, 97, 127, 6073, 28019), any product or quotient of two of them at most 10, and their largest exponent is 21. Fractions of plain integers can't do this: psi² takes 87 bits, (lbf·s)³ 130.

π, ln 2 and ln 10 are exact symbols beside the primes. ln 2 and ln 10 are there for the logarithmic units: a decibel is ln10/20 Np, a shannon ln 2 nat, a decade ln10/ln2 octaves. The square root of a magnitude halves its exponents (√(mHz) is 10^(-3/2) Hz^½); the root of a half power (a quarter power) is MagnitudeError::NotSquare.

Markers¶

Rational<NUM, DEN> takes u64 parameters and is factored into primes at compile time (trial division up to 2¹⁶, then Miller-Rabin and Pollard's rho), so any fraction a datasheet writes works. A prime factor above u32::MAX doesn't compile; no unit definition has one. Besides the prefixes, Pow10<E> and Rational:

  • Pow2<E>: binary steps. The Q formats are aliases over it, with the unit defaulting to Ratio: Q15<U> is Fixed<U, Pow2<-15>, i16>, Q31<U> is Fixed<U, Pow2<-31>, i32>, UQ16_16<U> is Fixed<U, Pow2<-16>, u32>.
  • FullScale<V, BITS>: V / 2^BITS, one step of a converter whose 2^BITS codes span V (LSB = Vref / 2ᴺ). A 12-bit ADC at 3.3 V is Fixed<Volt, FullScale<Rational<33, 10>, 12>, u16>.
  • FullScaleMax<V, BITS>: V / (2^BITS - 1), where the largest code is V (a PWM duty, a DAC reaching its reference). FullScaleMax<One, 16> is Duty16's 1/65535.
  • Binary angles: Bam16 is Fixed<Turn, Pow2<-16>, u16> (65536 steps a turn), Bam32 the same over u32.

Conversions at compile time¶

Fixed::to::<P2, T2>() works out P / P2 as a constant. Between integer storages the factor must be a fraction of integers, and the multiply must fit a 64-bit integer when both storages have up to 32 bits, else 128 bits. If it doesn't, the conversion doesn't compile; it is never rounded. At run time the conversion is the multiply and divide by those constants: nothing between equal scales, a division in the storage's own width when the factor is 1/n, None when the result isn't whole or doesn't fit T2. Float storages scale by the factor, π included.

use exact_units::{Fixed, Q15, prefix::{FullScale, Milli, Pow2, Rational}, units::Volt};

let half: Q15 = Fixed::new(16384);
assert_eq!(half.to::<Pow2<-14>, i16>().map(Fixed::get), Some(8192));
type Adc = Fixed<Volt, FullScale<Rational<33, 10>, 12>, u16>;
assert_eq!(Adc::new(4096).to::<Milli, u16>().map(Fixed::get), Some(3300));
assert_eq!(Adc::new(1241).to::<Milli, u16>(), None); // 999.8 mV isn't whole

The ms → µs difference is a range check on the input (x ≤ 4294967) in place of a 64-bit umull and a check of its high word. The gate that keeps such pairs equal is TEST-29.

Magnitude::apply_int and apply_f64 do the same for a scale only known at run time (a Quantity's prefix): exact or None for integers.

What a magnitude is not¶

  • An offset: °C against K, gauge against absolute pressure, register formulas like raw/256 + 25 are points with an origin.
  • A level: dBm, dB SPL and pH take a logarithm of the value, not a factor (STD-67). Only the factor between two logarithmic units (dB in Np) is a magnitude.
  • A non-linear scale: AWG, Beaufort, mpg against L/100 km are named conversion functions (STD-75).
  • A factor known only at run time: a calibration or a currency rate is a value (ww_si::Quantity, or the application's), not a unit.

Memory and wire¶

A Magnitude is plain const data without lifetimes, 104 bytes, so a firmware can hold a &'static Magnitude (a Quantity's exact prefix, STD-59). Fixed only uses it at compile time: a Fixed field costs what the plain number costs.

On the wire (final_structure) every exponent is twice the exponent as an i8, the primes UNib32: pow10, then the rest behind one option bit, its π / ln2 / ln10 behind one more, one bit per empty prime slot. A decimal prefix is 2 bytes (Milli fa 00), Kibi 5 (00 80 14 00 00), a degree 11, 1/65535 13, the pound 16. A magnitude that isn't canonical (a 2 among the primes, a prime that isn't one, an empty rest written out) is refused on decode. The cases are stdlib.si.magnitude.* in the conformance corpus.
