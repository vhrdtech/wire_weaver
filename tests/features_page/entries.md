How the "Features" page (`docs/features_today.md`) is made from this file (DOC-9)

The page is generated by `tests/features_page`: `just features-page` writes it, `just test` fails when it is stale.
This file says what the page says; `FEATURES.md` says what is done. Every item of `FEATURES.md` that is done (✅),
done with a hardware check open (🧪) or in progress (🚧) needs a line here, and only those: the test names what is
missing or stale. Items not done yet are marked on the page by their status.

- `# Title` starts an area of the page; the lines up to its first level are the area's opening, one or two plain
  sentences on what the area is for.
- `## Basic`, `## Advanced`, `## Nomicon` start a reading level (`docs/levels.md`), Basic first.
- `- IDS | name | line | link` is one item: one or more `FEATURES.md` IDs (`SW-31, SW-32`), a short name, one line
  on what it gives you, and a page under `docs/` (with an anchor) where it is explained; leave the link out when
  there is none. The name and line are Markdown.
- `# Omitted` lists items deliberately left off the page: `- IDS | reason`.

Everything above the first `#` is this description.

# Wire format

`shrink_wrap` turns your Rust structs and enums into compact bytes and back, without an allocator and without
copying. Every message between host and device is made of it, and it works on its own as a dense storage format.

## Basic

- SW-1 | Structs and enums | Your own structs, plain enums and enums that carry data, on the wire. | serdes/derive.md
- DER-1 | One derive attribute | `#[derive_shrink_wrap(..)]` with a few clear options and error messages that say what is wrong. | serdes/derive.md
- SW-2 | `Option` and `Result` | Stored as a single flag bit, also inside lists. | types.md#option-and-result
- SW-3 | Strings and lists | `String` and `Vec<T>`, `Vec<u8>` as plain bytes. | types.md#strings-and-lists
- SW-4 | Arrays and tuples | Fixed-size arrays, tuples and `()`. | types.md
- SW-59 | Tuple and unit structs | `struct Celsius(f32);` works like a named struct, with the same bytes; `struct Marker;` keeps room to grow fields later. | types.md#tuple-structs
- SW-5 | Integers of any width | `u1` to `u32` and nibbles, packed bit after bit, so a 3-bit field takes 3 bits. | types.md#booleans-and-numbers
- SW-6 | Variable-length numbers | `UNib32` and `UVlq32`, where small values take a nibble or a byte. | types.md
- SW-7 | Zero-copy reads | Strings and lists are read in place from the received buffer; an owned twin of each type is generated for values you keep. | serdes/derive.md#borrowed-owned-or-both
- SW-8, DER-3, SW-15 | Evolvable by default | Add fields later with `#[default = ..]`, and old and new code still read each other's data. | serdes/derive.md#evolving-types
- SW-16, SW-47 | Enum variants from a newer version | An older reader takes an unknown variant as `Unknown(u32)` instead of failing. | serdes/derive.md#enum-variants-from-a-newer-version
- SW-19 | Bounded lists and strings | `MaxLen<T, N>` holds at most `N` elements, checked both ways, with a worst-case size known at compile time. | types.md#bounded-lists-and-strings-maxlent-n
- SW-14 | Usable on its own | A dense format for storing many small objects, no API needed. | serdes/use_cases.md

## Advanced

- SW-57 | Owned values without an allocator | `ArrayString<N>` and `ArrayVec<T, N>` on fixed arrays, same bytes as `String` and `Vec`. | serdes/special_types.md#arraystring-arrayvec-owned-values-without-an-allocator
- SW-48 | Bit-sized fields and payloads | `U7`, `U10`, `I2` and friends in structs, enum variants and method arguments, e.g. a 7- or 10-bit I2C address in 8 or 11 bits. | serdes/showcase.md#bit-sized-fields-and-variant-payloads-a-7-or-10-bit-i2c-address
- SW-33 | 64-bit variable-length numbers | `UVlq64`, 1 to 10 bytes. | types.md
- SW-31, SW-32, SW-50 | Compressed number sequences | `Delta` and `DeltaOfDelta` for timestamps and counters, `XorFloat` for slowly changing floats, anywhere in an API. | serdes/special_types.md#delta-deltaofdelta-and-xorfloat-compressed-sequences
- SW-60 | Columnar series | `Series<T>`: a list of any struct written field by field, with deltas and bit packing per field, several times smaller than a `Vec<T>` of samples. |
- DER-13 | Sparse structs | A struct of optional fields stored as an `OptionAnyVec`, for "only what changed" updates. | serdes/layouts.md#sparse-a-struct-of-optional-fields
- SW-58 | Checks on read | `validate = path` rejects out-of-range values as they are read. | serdes/derive_reference.md#validate-path
- DER-12 | `Display` from doc comments | Error enums print their doc lines, no hand-written `Display`. | serdes/derive_reference.md#display
- DER-8 | `#[cfg]` on enum variants | Compile a variant out; it keeps its number on the wire. | serdes/derive_reference.md#cfg-on-enum-variants
- DER-2 | Checked discriminants | Variant numbers must fit the chosen `ww_repr`, implicit ones count like in Rust. | serdes/derive_reference.md#ww_repr-repr-enums-only
- DER-10, DER-11 | Generic field types | `Wrapper<'i, T>` and types from `macro_rules` work as fields. | serdes/derive.md#type-mapping
- SW-11 | Growing writer | `BufWriterOwned` for hosts, when the size isn't known up front. | serdes/shrink_wrap.md
- SW-12 | Writing in place | Builders that write a value straight into the output buffer, and patching a field after the rest is written. | serdes/showcase.md
- SW-44, SW-45 | Fixed layouts | Types with a fixed byte layout get fields at constant offsets, read and written without a parser, and a matching C header. | serdes/layouts.md#fixed_layout-fields-at-fixed-offsets
- SW-46, SW-52, SW-54 | Fixed head, sequential tail | `head_tail`, a format of its own for small firmware, with views that read fields on demand and a writer per enum variant. | serdes/layouts.md#head_tail-a-fixed-head-and-a-sequential-tail
- SW-43 | `shrink_wrap` in C | A C99 reader and writer of the same format, no `malloc`. | c.md

## Nomicon

- SW-13 | Format documented bit by bit | How every type is laid out, and why. | serdes/wire_format.md
- SW-34, SW-36, SW-37, SW-38, SW-41 | Small code on small CPUs | About 20 % less code on Cortex-M0 and RISC-V for the same bytes on the wire. | serdes/code_size.md
- SW-53 | Number access tuned per CPU | Multi-byte numbers through one function on RISC-V, inline on Arm, whichever is smaller. | serdes/code_size.md
- SW-39 | Byte-wise `memcpy` | Measured savings and downsides of a tiny `memcpy` for very small targets. | serdes/code_size.md#build-settings
- SW-35 | Exact sizes of enums | `sized` enums report the discriminant plus one variant; variants of different sizes are a compile error. | serdes/size_assumptions.md#enums
- SW-40 | Strict bit reads | Malformed data near the end of a buffer is an error, never a misread. | serdes/wire_format.md
- SW-9, DER-4 | Bytes to the end | `TailBytes` takes the rest of the buffer, written without a length; only allowed last. | types.md#special-types
- SW-18 | Size of the rest of a value | `TailSize<N>`, filled in after writing, so a reader can skip a value or bound it. | serdes/special_types.md#tailsizen-the-size-of-the-rest-of-a-value
- SW-55 | Sparse slots | `OptionAnyVec`, a list of optional values of any types where only the present ones take space. | serdes/special_types.md#optionanyvec-sparse-slots-of-any-types
- SW-10 | Lists of either-or values | `EitherAnyVec` holds one of two types per element, the flags packed by eight, e.g. a list of results. | serdes/special_types.md#eitheranyvec

# API

You describe what a device offers as a Rust trait: methods to call, properties to read and write, streams of data
in either direction. WireWeaver generates the device's server and the host's client from it.

## Basic

- API-1 | Methods, properties, streams and sinks | All in one trait, with traits nested inside to group them. | api/overview.md
- API-11 | Sync or async | Async and sync servers; async, blocking and promise-style clients, from the same trait. | api/methods.md
- API-4 | Results and errors | Methods return a value, answer later or say "not implemented"; your own error types for methods and properties. | api/errors.md
- API-5 | Request context | Every handler knows who asked and can send events or answer later. | api/methods.md#handler-context
- API-2 | Arrays of resources | `led[]` or `motor[]`, one definition for many instances, with the valid indices asked from the device. | api/arrays.md
- API-3 | Shared traits | `impl Gpio`, `impl Uart` from the standard library, so common tools work with your device. | api/traits.md
- API-36 | Constants | `const NAME: T;` items, read once per connection and cached. | api/properties.md#const-items

## Advanced

- API-16 | Optional items | Leave out what a board doesn't have, with no flash spent, and clients know before they call. | api/optional_items.md
- API-32 | Error lists | Which errors each method can return, shown in docs and tools and checked in tests. | api/methods.md#error-lists
- API-31, API-35 | Stream functions | Open a stream with typed arguments and a result, like a method call. | api/streams.md#stream-functions
- API-38 | Observing properties | Ask a device to send changes on change or periodically, within a byte budget. | api/observing.md
- API-18 | Size analysis | Worst-case request and reply sizes as constants, checked at compile time; properties and streams are next. | types.md#worst-case-size-at-compile-time
- DOC-6 | API docs in rustdoc | `#[ww_trait]` also leaves a plain Rust trait, so methods, properties and streams show up on docs.rs. | api/traits.md#in-rustdoc
- API-8 | Code generation from `build.rs` | `gen_server` / `gen_client` as an alternative to the macros. |
- API-26 | Generated C | The C runtime is there; generated C for an API's types and server is next. | c.md

## Nomicon

- API-6 | Builders for events and errors | Written straight into the output buffer. |
- API-7 | Dispatch on index slices | Requests are routed by `match` on their path, the base of multi requests. | api/addressing.md
- API-9, API-10 | Names and paths that don't collide | Generated names carry the whole trait chain; type paths come from Cargo. | api/folder_structure.md
- API-30 | Counted and reliable streams | Exact counts of lost items; acknowledgements, resends and resume after a reconnect. | api/stream_reliability.md
- API-28 | Credit sinks | Flow control for data sent to a device, so its buffer never overflows, and lossy links resume where they stopped. | api/credit_sinks.md
- API-20 | Trait paths | Call a shared trait wherever it sits in a device's API. | api/addressing.md

# Device side

The server is the code on the device that answers requests. It is generated from the trait, needs neither `std` nor an
allocator and does no IO itself, so it runs on any MCU and RTOS over the transport you choose.

## Basic

- SRV-1 | `no_std`, no allocator | The generated server works on the smallest MCUs and does no IO itself. | api/methods.md#server-side
- SRV-2 | Your event loop | An async `Server` with `wait()` / `handle()` inside your own loop, or a blocking one. | transport/overview.md
- SRV-6, SRV-7 | Media | USB (embassy), RTT, WebSocket and UDP, the last two also on `embassy-net`. | transport/overview.md
- SRV-5 | Device descriptors | Serial number, API name, version and a user label, visible before connecting. | transport/usb.md

## Advanced

- SRV-20 | Deferred replies everywhere | Answer a call later, also on nested trait levels. | api/methods.md#deferred
- SRV-18 | Observed properties | The device sends property changes within the budget each host asked for. | api/observing.md#the-device-side
- SRV-19 | ECC-safe STM32 flash | Reads a flash word broken by a power cut as zeros instead of crashing. | api/ecc_flash.md

## Nomicon

- SRV-3, SRV-4 | Split send and receive | Independent TX and RX paths for every transport; handlers called straight from the receive loop. |
- SRV-15 | Stream stores | Data a host hasn't acknowledged yet, kept in RAM or in a power-cut safe log on SD card or flash. | api/stream_stores.md
- SRV-16 | Logs that survive a crash | A store in RAM that outlives a reset, for what happened just before a fault. | api/stream_stores.md

# Transports

How the bytes get from one end to the other. Small messages are packed into one packet and big ones split, and a link
layer checks on connect that both ends speak the same protocol.

## Basic

- TR-3 | USB, RTT, WebSocket, UDP | And in-process, also as simulated USB for tests. | transport/overview.md
- TR-4 | USB network adapter | The device shows up as a USB network card with its own DHCP server, for WebSocket or UDP without drivers. | transport/websocket.md

## Advanced

- TR-1 | Framer | Packs messages into packets with 1 byte of overhead, CRC and recovery from lost frames. | transport/ww_framer.md
- TR-2 | Link layer | Link setup, device info and protocol version exchange. | transport/ww_link.md
- TR-12 | USB throughput on small MCUs | Measured and tuned on an STM32G0 so far. | transport/usb_internals.md
